The AI Doesn't Teach You. It Studies You.

Extuter, built by the Genartml team, goes live on October 3, 2026.

Team Genartml2 September 2026 · 9 min read
LinkedInX
In this article

    Most of what Genartml builds sits quietly inside other companies. Workflow automations for a real estate group. A custom AI assistant for an agritech founder. A chatbot that answers 4,000 queries a week for a healthcare brand. Good work, but invisible work.

    Every so often we build something of our own. Extuter is that.

    On October 3, we're launching Extuter — an AI study companion for JEE and NEET aspirants — as a full consumer product under our own roof. This post is the story of what it is, why we built it, and what it says about the kind of AI work we take on for clients.

    The problem we kept seeing

    There are already a hundred apps that will hand a JEE or NEET aspirant more content. More videos. More question banks. More PDFs. The problem was never a shortage of material. Every serious aspirant has more than they can finish in two years.

    The actual problem is that no one is watching how a student learns.

    A coaching teacher has 200 other students. Parents can't tell a rotational mechanics gap from a thermodynamics one. And the app the student is using has no memory of the fact that they keep dropping marks on the same three chapters, in the same kind of question, at the same point in a 3-hour paper.

    That gap — between the content a student consumes and the model no one is keeping of how they actually perform — is what Extuter fills.

    What Extuter actually is

    Extuter is not another content library. The content is the cheapest part of the stack.

    What it does instead is build a private, month-by-month model of the individual student. Every question attempted, every one they got wrong, the exact step they slipped at, how long they took, what time of day it was, which chapters they avoid, which mock day they crashed on. It keeps this quietly in the background while the student uses the app normally.

    After a few weeks, the app starts to know things about the student's prep that the student doesn't. It knows the difference between a chapter they understand and a chapter they can do under time pressure. It knows which mistakes are careless and which ones are conceptual. It knows the exact 40 minutes of a mock where their accuracy falls off a cliff.

    That model is the product. Everything else is a surface on top of it.

    Mistake DNA — the feature we're most proud of

    We call it Mistake DNA. Every wrong answer is not stored as "wrong" — it's tagged. Concept gap. Formula error. Reading mistake. Time pressure. Silly slip. Sign error. Unit error. Wrong assumption.

    After a few hundred attempts, patterns emerge that no student notices on their own. Things like — you get 82% of your electrostatics questions right when you attempt them in the first hour of a mock, and 41% right in the third hour. That isn't a concept problem. That's a stamina problem. The fix for it is completely different from more revision.

    Most aspirants spend two years mistaking one kind of problem for another. That is the single biggest reason smart kids don't crack top ranks. Mistake DNA is the first honest attempt we've seen at breaking that loop.

    Why we launched JEE first

    NEET is going online from next year. That shift is going to be brutal for a lot of aspirants who prepared their whole life on paper. We're building a full CBT simulator for it — pixel-faithful to the NTA interface, ugly on purpose, with a screen-stamina program that actually trains a student for a 3-hour on-screen paper instead of pretending it's the same as pen-and-paper.

    But JEE Main is already computer-based. So Mistake DNA, adaptive practice, and the Mock Analyst — the parts of Extuter that don't depend on CBT — ship first, for JEE aspirants preparing for the January and April attempts. The NEET variant plus CBT simulator ships in the next phase.

    What Extuter says about the way Genartml builds

    There's a reason we're telling this story on the Genartml site and not only on Extuter's own channels.

    Extuter is a working demonstration of the AI work we take on for clients. The same pattern shows up whether we're building a study companion for teenagers or an internal operations agent for a mid-market company:

    The data model is the moat, not the interface. Anyone can wrap an LLM in a chat window. Very few teams design the underlying schema that makes an AI system get sharper the longer it's used. That's the part we spend most of our time on. AI-only, not AI-flavoured. Extuter has no human tutors in the loop. Every doubt, every plan, every analysis is generated. That's a deliberate constraint, and it forces the system to be actually good rather than propped up by ops. Ship the boring parts. The admin console, the safety guardrails on the mentor AI, the fallback logic when a doubt is ambiguous — those are the parts that decide whether an AI product survives in production. Every client build we do puts the same weight on them.

    If any of that maps to a system you've been trying to get built inside your own company, that's exactly the kind of work our team does.

    What happens on October 3

    We're not doing a soft launch. October 3 is a real launch event — hybrid, in-person and livestream. Signups open the same day. The first cohort of aspirants from the closed beta will already have their student models running, and their stories will be part of what we show.

    If you know a JEE or NEET aspirant, send them to extuter.com on launch day.

    If you're a founder or operator who wants an AI system built the way Extuter was built — with a real data model underneath and no shortcuts on the boring parts — reach out at daksh@genartml.com.

    October 3, 2026.
